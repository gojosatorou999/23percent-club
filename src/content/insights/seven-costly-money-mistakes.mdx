The expensive mistakes in personal finance are rarely dramatic. They're ordinary choices, repeated, that compound against you. Here are seven we see most often.

## 1. Buying insurance as an investment

Endowment and money-back plans promise safety and returns. Usually they deliver too little cover and returns of 4 to 6% a year. A term plan for protection, plus a separate investment for growth, almost always does better on both.

## 2. Withdrawing EPF when changing jobs

It feels like a bonus. It's a tax-efficient, 8%-plus debt investment you'll struggle to replace, broken for a new phone or a holiday. Transfer it instead.

## 3. Carrying a credit card balance

At 36 to 45% a year, a revolving balance costs more than any investment can reliably earn. Paying the minimum due keeps the card “current” and your debt growing.

## 4. No emergency fund

Without one, every surprise becomes debt or a forced sale of investments, often at a bad time. Three to six months of essential expenses, kept liquid, protects everything else.

## 5. Stopping SIPs when markets fall

The months you pause are the months your SIP would have bought the most units. The fall doesn't damage a long-term plan; stepping away from it does.

## 6. Trading futures and options for income

SEBI's study of FY 2022 to FY 2024 found that about 93% of individual F&O traders lost money, with aggregate losses of over ₹1.8 lakh crore. It's not a side income; for most people it's a slow leak.

<Callout>
If something is promoted as easy income, ask who earns when you trade. Usually it's the platform and the other side of your trade.
</Callout>

## 7. Waiting to start

The most expensive mistake is also the most common: waiting until you earn more, understand more or the market “settles”. Ten years of delay can cut what a SIP is worth at retirement by more than half.

None of these needs a financial genius to fix. They need a few rules, set once and kept.
