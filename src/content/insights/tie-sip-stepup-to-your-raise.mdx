Most financial resolutions fail for a boring reason: they ask you to make the same hard decision every month, forever. "I'll save more" is a decision you have to keep making. A step-up SIP is a decision you make exactly once.

## The mechanism, not the motivation

A step-up SIP does one thing: it increases your monthly contribution by a fixed percentage, automatically, once a year. You set it up alongside your regular SIP mandate through the same route — most mutual fund platforms and AMCs (Asset Management Companies) in India support it as a standard feature, not a special product.

That's the whole trick. You're not relying on remembering to increase your SIP every year, or on having the discipline to actually do it when the moment comes. The increase is already scheduled. It happens whether or not you're thinking about your finances that month.

## Why timing it to your raise works

Here's the part that makes it psychologically painless rather than a sacrifice: if your step-up percentage is roughly in line with your typical annual raise, you never actually take home less money than you did the year before — you just take home a smaller increase. That's a very different feeling from cutting your current spending.

A flat SIP that never grows is quietly losing ground to your own rising income. Ten years in, the same monthly amount represents a shrinking share of what you earn. A step-up SIP keeps your investing roughly proportional to your income instead of proportional to the number you happened to pick when you were younger and earning less.

<Callout>
This only works if the step-up is automatic. A step-up you have to manually trigger every year is really just twelve more monthly decisions in disguise, plus one annual one — you've added a task, not removed one.
</Callout>

## The number that makes the case

The honest way to see whether this is worth setting up is to compare two versions of yourself: one who keeps the same monthly SIP for the entire duration, and one who steps it up every year by the same percentage as their raise. The gap between those two outcomes at maturity is usually the single most persuasive number in personal finance, because it isn't asking you to invest more of your current income — it's asking you to invest the same *share* of a bigger number.

Set it up once. Let the calendar do the rest.
